# Course Supporter

The Course Supporter platform makes good learning more accessible. The most expensive part of any course is reviewing each student's work individually. The platform takes that on: tests are checked by the system, and homework is reviewed automatically by the Mentor — a program that reads the work in the context of the course and explains what worked and what is worth fixing, with recommendations and comments. No person takes part in grading.

Course Supporter provides two software services: homework review and course material processing. You pay with prepaid packages. Courses are created by authors; the rights to the materials stay with them. The platform does not sell access to courses and does not charge for it. A student pays for the review of their own work, and an author pays for processing the materials of their course. The course author decides how students get into the course and whether it is open to everyone: they open it to all or give access to students individually.

A package is a set number of units, that is, an amount of prepaid work by the platform. A unit is not money and not a means of payment: it cannot be exchanged for money, transferred to another person or sold. For the unused part of a valid package, we refund the payment — see [Refunds](https://pythoncourse.me/en/refunds/).

Packages and prices are on the [pricing page](https://pythoncourse.me/en/pricing/), and the rules of use are in the [Terms of Use](https://pythoncourse.me/en/terms/).

## For course authors {#authors}

You lay out your course the way you have it — videos, presentations, texts, assignments and tests. Then two assistants get to work; both are programs.

**The Methodist** reads the materials and writes a description of each section: what it teaches, what the student has to show in their work, which mistakes are the most common. Along the way, it notes gaps and inconsistencies in the materials — from its own point of view; what to do about them is up to you. You can see the descriptions and edit them — put the emphasis on what you consider most important — because the Mentor works from them afterwards.

**The Mentor** automatically reviews submitted work in the context of your particular course: against the section descriptions and the assignment's criteria. This way, every piece of your students' work gets a detailed review with explanations, and you do not review each one by hand. You can directly influence how the Mentor assesses work: upload your own methodological materials, from which the Methodist writes the section descriptions, and edit the descriptions themselves — they act as the course's internal teaching guide, and the Mentor reviews by them. This is configuring a program, not grading by hand: no person takes part in grading.

How to prepare a course — in the [For authors](authors/index.md) section. The author app is at `panel.pythoncourse.me`; the access key for it is issued by the Course Supporter platform administrator: write to support@pythoncourse.me.

## For students {#students}

If the author of your course runs it on the Course Supporter platform, you submit homework and take tests in the student portal. Every piece of work you submit gets a review generated automatically by the Mentor — a program, not a person: what worked, what is worth fixing and how. You do the work yourself: the Mentor reviews it and explains the grade; it does not solve the assignment for you.

The Mentor runs on a large language model trained on a very large body of texts from many fields of knowledge. So it can connect your work to the wider context of the field, but it assesses it first of all against the materials of this particular course and the guidance of its author.

The platform does not sell courses: there is no charge for access to a course. You pay for the review of your work with a prepaid package: the cost of a review depends on the size and complexity of the work, and the platform shows the estimated cost before it starts.

Access to a course is given by its author: they send you a link to the portal, a login and a password.
